How to read this list

Score 90+ and entry ready is the gate, not the edge. The score measures how violent the premarket has been, not which way the day goes - high scorers have the same odds of breaking the premarket high as everyone else and a worse median drawdown. What separated the winners from the losers on the 27 backfilled 08:00-rule trades (Jun 1 - Sep 24, 2026) was in the other columns. Winners: 18 of 27. All numbers below are medians from that set and are a hypothesis being tested forward, not a rule.

Rank
Score first, then premarket dollar volume. Rank 1 at 08:00 is the only name the 08:00 rule trades, bought at the 08:30 open with no re-scoring. Everything else on the page is context for that one decision.
Score
Out of 100: gap, participation (capped - see turnover), structure (AVWAP, EMA pair, distance from the premarket high, coil), catalyst, spread. Winners and losers both scored a median 94. Read 90 as "eligible", not "good". Under 90, or not entry ready, the rule does not trade.
Profile
The winner-profile read. Fits = float under 3M shares and first morning on the list; those two alone kept 16 of the 27 trades, 14 of them winners, $7,008 against $6,117 for all 27. Watch = one of those two failed: 5 of 9 losers had floats over 3M, and all four second-morning names closed down 25% or more. Fits, but watch = the hard reads pass but a softer tilt is present (rotation under 10x, price under $5, more than 12% under the premarket high, or float unknown). Drawn from the same 27 trades it is scored on, so treat it as a lean, not a law.
Price
The last premarket trade. Under $5, 5 of 10 lost; $5 and over, 4 of 17. Winners median $9.00, losers $4.70. Sizing is $2,000 a trade in whole shares, so a $3 name is 660 shares of a thin book.
Gap
From the last regular-session close. Size is not edge: losers gapped more (median +68%) than winners (+57%), and the +424% name lost. What mattered was whether the gap held into the open - 11 of 18 winners opened at or above their 08:00 price, only 3 of 9 losers did. Hover for the previous close and session.
PM volume / $ volume
Shares and dollars traded since 03:00 CT. Winners median $170M, losers $104M. The score's participation block caps at $20M, so a $30M name and a $300M name score the same - this column tells you what the score cannot.
Turnover
Premarket volume as a multiple of the float - how many times the float has changed hands. Winners 17x, losers 7.5x; every big-float loser rotated under 11x. The score caps this at 0.5x, so 1x and 30x score identically. Under 10x is a watch note.
Spread
Bid-ask as a percent of price. Pure cost: it is paid on the way in and the way out, and the executor refuses names over its limit. No separation between winners and losers, but wide spreads make the stop fill worse.
RSI
14-period on 5-minute bars. Winners 63, losers 65 - no information about direction here. It is on the page because a reading over 80 at 08:00 usually means the move already happened overnight.
Coil
The last 30 minutes' range as a multiple of ATR; lower is tighter. Earns structure points but did not separate the groups (2.8 both). A tight coil into the open is comfortable to hold, not more likely to run.
Trend
The four readiness checks: above premarket AVWAP, EMA 9 over 20, within 3% of the premarket high, and the coil. Three of four are required. The one that carried information was distance from the premarket high: losers sat a median 12.7% under it at 08:00, winners 6.9%; over 12% under, 5 of 8 lost. "Away from PMH" on a rank-1 name is the thing to look at twice.
Levels
The five numbers to have written down before the open, and which side of them the last premarket price sits on: prior day high (PDH), prior day low (PDL), prior close (PC), premarket high (PMH), premarket low (PML). Taken from a study of a 0DTE options trader's alerts (RAKEN_SINGLE_NAMES.md): every alert was a level from this list, direction from which side price was on, entry when the open printed through it, strike on the next level. Above all = call candidate if the 09:30 print holds above; Below all = put candidate; Inside = nothing at the open, wait for two one-minute closes through PMH or PML. On a year of seven large caps a break closed on its side 52-62% of the time and reached the next level 69-82%: reach is the reliable part, direction is a lean. A read, not a gate; the stock executor ignores it.
Float
The strongest single column. 16 of 18 winners under 3M shares; 5 of 9 losers over 3M, and the only big-float winner had $227M behind it. Today's float from TradingView, not the float on the day of an older record. Unknown float is unknown, not small.
Catalyst / news
A verified headline is required for entry ready; a cash buyout at a fixed price is vetoed (the stock is pinned and cannot run). Catalyst type did not separate winners from losers - earnings, mergers, contracts and FDA news all appear on both sides. What to look for is the other direction: an offering, an S-1 or a 424B in the last few days is dilution that a headline will not mention.

After the open - what a winner looks like, and why you do not act on it early

Every pick, winners included, trades below the 08:30 open in the first five minutes, so a dip is not a signal. Winners: a green first minute, back above the open by minute 5, a first-five-minute dip under 3%, and opening volume that matches the premarket - the first five minutes carried a fifth of everything that traded premarket (12 of 18 winners over 15%; 1 of 9 losers). Ten of the 18 hit +10% inside three minutes; the rest ground higher for hours - the day's low inside the first 30 minutes, the high 1 to 6 hours later. Losers are the mirror: thin opening volume, the opening print is the high for 6 of 9, and the low keeps coming all day.

When winners peak. Median 100 minutes after the open, about 10:10 CT. Only 2 of 18 topped inside the first 30 minutes and both were small; 6 peaked between 09:00 and 10:00, 5 between 10:00 and 11:30, and 5 after 11:30 (FGI at 11:37, TNON at 13:48, NBIL at 14:31). By minute 30 a winner has typically shown half of its eventual peak, by minute 60 seven tenths; 11 of 18 had peaked by 10:30. Stalls are normal on the way - MEDS made no new high from minute 18 to minute 60, then doubled. From the peak, winners give back a median 23% by the close, which is what the 25% trail is sized to. Losers peak at minute 2.

None of that is an exit rule. Selling anything still under the open at minute 10, 30 or 60 lost money at every check because the grinders (FGI, AGEN, NBIL, SDOT) dip 6-9% first and then pay the most. The exits are the ladder's job: a third out at +30%, the rest trailing 25% off the high, stop -20%, flat by 14:45 CT.
